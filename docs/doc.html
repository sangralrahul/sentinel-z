01 · Executive summary

AI on the most sensitive data a bank has, without exposing any of it

Login, transfer and download logs are where account takeovers, mule networks and data theft first show up, and they are full of personal data. Shipping them to an external AI service creates exactly the exposure a security team exists to prevent. Sentinel-Z removes identity before analysis and keeps the analysis next to the data: a zero-dependency engine that runs identically in a browser tab, on a server, or on Linux on IBM Z / LinuxONE.

What the solution delivers

Privacy by construction

Keyed HMAC-SHA256 pseudonyms for every identifier, PII masking in free text (email, phone, Luhn-checked cards, Aadhaar, PAN, IP), and differential privacy (ε = 1) for anything shared outside the security team.

Detection that explains itself

A from-scratch Isolation Forest combined with expert rules catches seven attack types. Every alert carries plain-English reasons and MITRE ATT&CK techniques, grouped into incidents.

An analyst's console

Triage workflow, customer risk timelines, day × hour heat map, a red-team attack simulator, a what-if threshold slider, and a printable, pseudonymised incident report.

Built for IBM Z

No native modules and no dependencies, so it runs unchanged on linux/s390x. A multi-arch container is built and tested for s390x in CI; optional summaries come from IBM Granite running locally.

02 · Problem and architecture

The privacy paradox in security analytics

Why existing approaches fall short

  • Cloud AI services need the raw logs, moving customer data outside the bank's control and across borders.
  • Static rule engines are private but blind to anything nobody wrote a rule for, such as quiet insider misuse.
  • Black-box scores are hard to act on and hard to defend to auditors and regulators.
  • Plain hashing of identifiers is reversible by hashing a list of known emails or phone numbers.

Design principles

  1. Remove identity first. Nothing downstream ever sees a raw identifier.
  2. Learn, don't label. Baselines come from history, so no labelled attacks are needed.
  3. Explain every alert. Reasons an analyst can verify in seconds.
  4. Run where the data lives. Zero dependencies, any architecture, no network.
  5. Measure honestly. Publish the metrics and the limits.

03 · Privacy design

Four layers of protection, each tested on every commit

1Keyed pseudonymisation

Each identifier becomes prefix_ + first 12 hex of HMAC-SHA256(key, kind ‖ value). The same customer always maps to the same token under one key, so behaviour can be learned, but without the key the token cannot be reversed or matched against a list of known emails.

priya.sharma4@bank.example
→ usr_8506020624e6

2PII redaction in free text

Notes are scanned for emails, phone numbers (including spaced formats), card numbers (validated with the Luhn checksum so invoice numbers survive), Aadhaar, PAN and IP addresses.

"call +91 98765 43210, card 4111 1111 1111 1111"
→ "call [PHONE], card [CARD]"

3Differential privacy for sharing

Counts sent to auditors or other teams (events, alerts, incidents, alerts per signal) carry Laplace noise with ε = 1. Exact figures never leave the security team.

4Proof of no exfiltration

No component calls a cloud API. The website ships a Content-Security-Policy with connect-src 'self', so the browser itself refuses to send data elsewhere, and the page counts its own external requests live (always 0).

Key management

The key is never written to disk. In the browser it lives in memory; on servers it comes from SENTINEL_KEY via the bank's secret manager. The UI shows only an 8-character fingerprint, and a 16-character minimum is enforced.

Threat model

ThreatMitigation
Model or analyst sees customer identitiesPseudonyms only; re-identification needs the key and the bank's own customer records
Dictionary attack on tokensKeyed HMAC rather than a plain hash; key length enforced
PII hidden in free textPattern redaction with checksum validation
Logs sent to an AI providerNo cloud calls; optional LLM restricted to localhost or private networks

04 · Detection model

A hybrid that learns normal and knows known attacks

Each customer's first window of history (14 days by default) builds a baseline: usual hour of activity (circular mean), typical transfer and download sizes, known devices, cities and payees. Every later event is described by ten features that measure departure from that baseline or bursts in short windows.

FeatureMeaningCatches
hour_devHours from the customer's usual timeOff-hours takeover, insiders
amount_zTransfer size against the customer's normalFraudulent payments
bytes_zDownload size against the customer's normalData exfiltration
new_deviceDevice never seen in the baselineAccount takeover
new_cityCity never seen in the baselineTakeover, travel
travel_speedSpeed implied by previous city (haversine)Impossible travel
fails_10mFailed logins in the last 10 minutesBrute force
ip_users_1hDistinct customers on one IP in the last hourCredential stuffing
txn_10mTransfers in the last 10 minutesMule transfers
new_beneficiaryPayment to an account never paid beforeTakeover, mules

Unsupervised model

An Isolation Forest written from scratch (150 trees, sample size 256, seeded for reproducibility) is fitted on baseline events only. Its anomaly score is normalised between the baseline median and 99.9th percentile.

Expert evidence

Known indicators (impossible travel, brute force, shared IP, large amounts, bursts, large downloads, new payee, new device or city, odd hour) carry weights and combine as independent evidence.

Hybrid risk and threshold

rules = 1 − ∏ (1 − wᵢ / 12)
risk = 1 − (1 − 0.85·model)(1 − rules)

The alert threshold is the 99.5th percentile of baseline risk (never below 0.5), so it adapts to each bank instead of being a magic number. Sensitivity presets: strict 99.8, balanced 99.5, sensitive 99.0.

Severity and incidents

Critical: risk ≥ 0.9 or a high-weight indicator. High: risk ≥ 0.75. Otherwise medium. Alerts for one customer within 2 hours form a single incident.

Why hybrid: rules are excellent at known patterns but blind to the unexpected; an unsupervised model sees novelty but cannot spot a pattern it never varies on in training (for example, a brand-new city). Together they cover each other's gaps, and the ablation on the next page shows it.

07 · IBM Z deployment and engineering

Runs where the bank already runs

Core banking lives on IBM Z. Because Sentinel-Z is plain JavaScript with zero dependencies and no native modules, the exact same engine runs on linux/s390x beside the systems of record. Nothing needs to be copied to a cloud AI service, and the platform's isolation, encryption and audit controls apply automatically.

$ docker build -t sentinel-z . # multi-arch, tests run during build
$ docker run --rm -p 5173:5173 sentinel-z # web console
$ SENTINEL_KEY=… node cli/sentinel-z.js analyze logs.csv --train-days 14 --out report.json
$ ollama pull granite3.3:2b && node cli/sentinel-z.js analyze logs.csv --explain

Quality gates

14 automated tests: crypto against Node's reference, redaction, model behaviour, end-to-end accuracy thresholds, reproducibility, simulator, CLI, and a check that no raw email or internal IP appears in any output.

Continuous integration

GitHub Actions runs the suite and the demo on Node 18, 20 and 22, then builds and tests the container for linux/s390x under QEMU on every push.

Secure hosting

Static deployment with strict CSP, no-referrer, frame denial and nosniff headers. The image runs as a non-root user. MIT licence; self-hosted IBM Plex fonts.

Scaling path on IBM Z

StageApproach
TodayBatch or interactive scoring at ≈ 20,000 events per second per thread; files of any size via the CLI
StreamingScore events as they arrive from z/OS or LinuxONE log streams; rolling baselines per customer
AccelerationExport the forest for inference on the IBM Z Integrated Accelerator for AI (Telum) for in-transaction scoring
FeedbackAnalyst decisions (false positive, resolved) become known-good patterns that tune thresholds per segment

Data sets used

A synthetic banking security log generated by the project (core/generator.js): 80 customers, 21 days, about 6,000 login, failed-login, transfer and download events with IP, device, city, amount, bytes, beneficiary account and free-text notes. Attacks and legitimate surprises are injected with ground-truth labels and campaign IDs. No real customer data is used; the generator is seeded, so every result is reproducible. The engine accepts any CSV or JSON log with timestamp, user, event columns, and is ready for public datasets such as LANL authentication logs.

08 · Audience, limitations and roadmap

Who it serves and what comes next

AudienceValue
Bank SOC analystsPrioritised, explained incidents with ATT&CK mapping and triage, without seeing identities
Fraud and risk teams (banks, NBFCs, payment firms)Early detection of account takeover, mule transfers and impossible travel
CISOs, DPOs, complianceAI on sensitive logs that respects privacy law (e.g. India's DPDP Act) and data-localisation expectations
IBM Z platform teamsDetection deployed beside core banking data, no data movement
Auditors and partnersDifferentially private summaries for oversight without exposing individuals

Known limitations

  • Evaluated on synthetic data only.
  • Baselines are static per run.
  • City-level location; no ASN or VPN signals yet.
  • Pseudonyms hide identity, not behaviour; very distinctive patterns can hint at a person, which is why external sharing uses differential privacy.

Roadmap

  1. Validate on LANL and partner logs inside the bank.
  2. Rolling baselines and analyst feedback loop.
  3. Streaming ingestion from z/OS / LinuxONE.
  4. Inference on the IBM Z Integrated Accelerator for AI.
  5. ASN, VPN and device-fingerprint signals.
